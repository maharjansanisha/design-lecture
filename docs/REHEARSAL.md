# Rehearsal guide

You have 30 minutes. The scripted speaking in the notes takes about **11 minutes**. The rest goes to student answers, the 45-second activity, pauses and Q&A. That buffer is deliberate: interactive sessions almost always run longer than you expect.

## Three rehearsals

**1. Read-through (about 40 min).** Open the deck with `N` notes. Say each slide out loud in your own words. Don't memorise. Mark any sentence that feels unnatural and rewrite it in `js/notes.js`.

**2. Timed run (30 min).** Use the presenter window and start its timer. Pretend students answer by pausing for about 15 seconds at every "Ask the room" moment. Note where you go over.

**3. Full dress run.** If possible, do this in the actual room or with a friend as the audience. Use the projector, clicker and extended display. Check that the back row can read slide 7's table.

## If you're running late

Cut in this order, without rushing your voice:

1. **Slide 5**: skip the optional question and just narrate the loop. Saves about 1 min.
2. **Slide 7**: don't read each tool's description. Say the one-line difference and show the table. Saves about 1–2 min.
3. **Slide 4**: take two answers instead of three, and use the 30-second timer. Saves about 1 min.
4. **Slide 3**: skip the hands-up vote on the phone screens. Saves about 30 s.

**Never cut:** the question on slide 8, or the closing line on slide 9.

## If you're running early

- Take more answers on slides 2 and 4. Students' own examples are the best content you have.
- On slide 7, open one tool live and type a single prompt. Only do this if the Wi-Fi is reliable and you've tried it beforehand.
- Give Q&A more time.

## Delivery tips for a first lecture

- **Start slowly.** The first 60 seconds set your pace for the whole talk. Take a breath before the first sentence.
- **Silence is your friend.** After a question, count to five in your head before helping. It feels long to you, but students need it.
- **Repeat student answers** into the room. The back rows can't hear the front, and it shows the student you valued what they said.
- **Use names** when you can ("Good point, Aarav").
- **You don't need to know everything.** "That's a great question. I'm not sure, but here's how I'd find out" is a strong answer.
- **Look at the room, not the screen.** The presenter window shows you everything you need.
- **Hand over warmly** to the Git & Linux instructor at the end.
